What is simulated

Every neuron in the adult fly brain is a leaky integrate-and-fire unit wired exactly as in the FlyWire connectome: 139,255 neurons and 2.7 million connections, each weighted by its synapse count. Excitation or inhibition comes from each neuron's transmitter (acetylcholine excites; GABA, glutamate and histamine inhibit). Parameters follow Shiu et al. 2024, which showed this kind of model predicts real feeding and grooming circuits.

There are no behaviour rules. The world only sets the firing rates of sensory neurons, and the body only reads the firing rates of the descending and motor neurons listed below. If a lesion changes behaviour, it is because it changed how activity flows through the wiring.

Brain → body readout

The ventral nerve cord that turns descending commands into leg and wing movements is not part of the brain connectome, so it is replaced by this fixed table. It never looks at stimuli or lesions.

World → brain transduction

ChannelNeurons

Vision enters at the looming and small-object detectors of the lobula (LPLC2, LC4, LPLC1, LC6, LC11, LC10a). Each detector's receptive field comes from its photoreceptor inputs in the connectome. Pure-retina mode drives the 11,000 photoreceptors instead and leaves all visual processing to the model, which cannot compute motion well because it has no graded neurons.

Arousal depolarises every neuron equally. It stands in for the neuromodulatory state that makes real flies walk spontaneously, which the wiring diagram does not contain. Above ~3 mV the brain runs roughly 3× slower.

Known limits

Touch on the legs, wings and thorax is sensed in the ventral nerve cord, so only head, eye and antenna touch is available. Hunger, fatigue and learning are not modelled. Transmitter identities come from machine predictions (Eckstein et al. 2024) where no experiment exists, and some are wrong. In the intact model the fly stands still until something drives its walking neurons, because nothing in the wiring alone produces spontaneous walking.
